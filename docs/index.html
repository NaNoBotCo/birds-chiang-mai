The Coucal Clock

Next call–
Dawn–
Dusk–
Moon–
Lunar day–

A cuckoo clock whose bird is a greater coucal. It calls at civil dawn and civil dusk, the moments the sun stands 6° below the horizon, worked out fresh each day for San Sai, so the call drifts through the year the way a living coucal's does.

The clock is a pair of carved teak almanac clocks for a wat in San Sai. This is its face, redrawn for the web: Thai numerals, the Buddhist year, the lunar day counted from the published wan phra calendar, and a moon window.

The moon window works like the old German and Swiss dials. One disc carries two moons and turns half a circle each lunation behind two cloud humps. The proportions were fitted against the real moon: the lit area it shows is within 1.1 percentage points of the sky's, and the disc resets at every true new moon.

The birds

Each bird is drawn from a few dozen numbers: a superellipse body, a teardrop wing, a Bézier neck, spirals and stripes. Press play to hear a real recording; the bird opens its bill as loud as the sound is. The strip at the bottom of the screen is a spectrogram: pitch rises up the strip, brightness is loudness. Each card then counts its own recording, in your browser.

Greater Coucal

นกกระปูดใหญ่ · Centropus sinensis

nok kra-puut yai · 48 cm

Listen for
A deep, hollow poop-poop-poop, six to twenty notes, often a pair answering each other.
Fact
A cuckoo that raises its own chicks. It builds a domed grass nest deep in a thicket and walks the grass for snails, lizards and grasshoppers.
Story
Villages told the time by it: the coucal calls at first light and again at dusk. The Coucal Clock on this page keeps the same hours.

Asian Koel

นกกาเหว่า · Eudynamys scolopaceus

nok ka-wao · 43 cm

Listen for
A rising ko-EL, ko-EL that climbs and speeds up. Loudest in the hot season, often before dawn and on moonlit nights.
Fact
The male is glossy black with a red eye; the female is brown, spotted white. She lays her eggs in crows' nests, and the crows raise the chicks.
Story
A Thai lullaby begins “กาเหว่าเอย ไข่ให้แม่กาฟัก”: koel, you left your egg for the crow to hatch. The crow raises the chick as her own, until it flies away.

Oriental Magpie-Robin

นกกางเขนบ้าน · Copsychus saularis

nok kang-khen baan · 20 cm

Listen for
Rich, changing whistles from a roof or a wire, often the first song before dawn. It copies other birds.
Fact
The male holds his garden all year, tail cocked and flicking. It is the national bird of Bangladesh, where it is called doel.

Red-whiskered Bulbul

นกปรอดหัวโขนเคราแดง · Pycnonotus jocosus

nok prot hua-khon khrao daeng · 20 cm

Listen for
Bright, bouncy phrases (“pettigrew”, “kink-a-joo”) all day from the bushes.
Fact
Thai name หัวโขน: the tall crest looks like the pointed head-dress of a khon dancer.
Story
In the far south it is นกกรงหัวจุก, star of singing contests: rows of cages hung on poles, judged on voice and stamina. Wild birds are protected in Thailand; contest birds are bred under permit.

Doi Suthep–Pui National Park · Suan Buak Hat · Queen Sirikit Botanic Garden

Streak-eared Bulbul

นกปรอดหน้านวล · Pycnonotus conradi

nok prot naa nuan · 20 cm

Listen for
A sharp whit-whit and a scratchy chatter, all over town.
Fact
Plain brown with pale streaks over the ear, and one of the commonest birds in Chiang Mai gardens. A 2016 DNA study split it from its look-alike twin in Myanmar, the Ayeyarwady bulbul.
Places to try
Tha Phae Gate and the moat · Suan Buak Hat · Ang Kaew, Chiang Mai University

Common Myna

นกเอี้ยงสาริกา · Acridotheres tristis

nok iang sa-ri-ka · 25 cm

Listen for
Gurgles, clicks and whistles. A pair bob their heads and fluff up while they talk.
Fact
Pairs stay together, often for life. It walks instead of hopping. Carried abroad, it became one of the world's worst invasive species; here it is at home.
Story
Sarika is the talking myna of old Thai tales. Sarika charms are carried for a sweet tongue, so that people listen kindly.
Places to try
Tha Phae Gate and the moat · Warorot Market

Coppersmith Barbet

นกตีทอง · Psilopogon haemacephalus

nok tii thong · 16 cm

Listen for
tuk… tuk… tuk, on and on, like a smith beating metal. It turns its head with each note, so the sound seems to come from everywhere.
Fact
ตีทอง means beating gold. It chisels its nest hole in a dead branch and lives on figs.

Lineated Barbet

นกโพระดกธรรมดา · Psilopogon lineatus

nok pho-ra-dok tham-ma-da · 28 cm

Listen for
A loud, rolling poo-pooh or kotook, repeated from the top of a fig tree: one of the loudest sounds of the hot season.
Fact
A big barbet with a heavy yellow bill and a streaky brown head. It swallows figs whole and spreads the seeds.

Black Drongo

นกแซงแซวหางปลา · Dicrurus macrocercus

nok saeng-saeo haang plaa · 28 cm

Listen for
Harsh ti-tiu calls and scolding. A fine mimic.
Fact
Fearless: it chases crows and hawks from its nest, and small birds nest nearby for the protection. It rides on buffalo and follows grass fires to catch the insects that fly up.

Spotted Dove

นกเขาใหญ่ · Spilopelia chinensis

nok khao yai · 30 cm

Listen for
A soft cru-cru-crooo, the last note falling.
Fact
Both parents feed the chicks crop milk, a rich liquid made in the throat. Pigeons and doves are among the few birds that make milk.
Story
In the deep south, zebra doves (นกเขาชวา) sing in cooing contests from cages hoisted up tall poles.

Olive-backed Sunbird

นกกินปลีอกเหลือง · Cinnyris jugularis

nok kin-plii ok lueang · 11 cm

Listen for
Thin, sweet cheep notes and a quick trill.
Fact
กินปลี means banana-flower eater: it sips nectar from banana blossoms. It hangs a purse-shaped nest from eaves and wires, stitched with spider silk.

White-throated Kingfisher

นกกระเต็นอกขาว · Halcyon smyrnensis

nok kra-ten ok khao · 28 cm

Listen for
A loud, laughing rattle, kililili, often in flight.
Fact
It hunts far from water too: lizards, frogs, big insects, even small snakes. The blue is not pigment; the feathers' structure scatters blue light.

Large-tailed Nightjar

นกตบยุงหางยาว · Caprimulgus macrurus

nok top-yung haang yao · 31 cm

Listen for
tchonk… tchonk… tchonk, like an axe on wood, after dusk and sometimes all night.
Fact
Its Thai name means mosquito-slapper. By day it lies flat on the leaf litter, invisible; at night its huge gape scoops moths from the air. It lays its eggs on bare ground.

Asian Barred Owlet

Listen for
A rising, bubbling trill that grows louder and faster. It calls by day too.
Fact
A small owl with no ear tufts, barred all over, hunting by day as well as night: big insects, lizards, mice, small birds.

Common Tailorbird

นกกระจิบธรรมดา · Orthotomus sutorius

nok kra-jip tham-ma-da · 12 cm

Listen for
A loud, repeated towit-towit-towit, far bigger than the bird.
Fact
It sews. It pierces the edges of a big leaf with its bill, pulls spider silk or plant fibre through, and builds its nest inside the cone. Darzee in Kipling's “Rikki-Tikki-Tavi” is a tailorbird.

Eurasian Hoopoe

นกกะรางหัวขวาน · Upupa epops

nok ka-raang hua khwaan · 28 cm

Listen for
A soft, hollow hoop-hoop-hoop: three notes, and the bird's name in many languages.
Fact
Its crest folds flat into an axe (หัวขวาน, axe-head) and opens into a fan when it is excited. It probes the ground for grubs with its long bill.
Story
In the Quran and in Jewish lore the hoopoe is Solomon's messenger, the bird that brought news of the Queen of Sheba. In Attar's Persian poem The Conference of the Birds it leads all the birds on their journey to find their king.

Red Junglefowl

ไก่ป่า · Gallus gallus

kai paa · 70 cm

Listen for
A rooster's crow, cut short: the last note clipped off.
Fact
The wild ancestor of every chicken on Earth. A 2020 genome study traced the first chickens to the red junglefowl of southwestern China, northern Thailand and Myanmar.
Story
Thai children learn the tale of the young Prince Naresuan, a hostage in Hongsawadi, whose fighting cock beat the bird of the Burmese crown prince.

Doi Suthep–Pui National Park · Doi Inthanon

Green Peafowl

นกยูงไทย · Pavo muticus

nok yuung thai · 200 cm

Listen for
A loud, trumpeting ki-wao at dawn and dusk, carrying across valleys.
Fact
Endangered. Thailand holds one of the last strong populations, in the west and in the north around Phayao. Tap it: the train opens, its eyespots set on the golden-angle spiral that packs a sunflower's seeds.

Asian Openbill

นกปากห่าง · Anastomus oscitans

nok paak haang · 81 cm

Listen for
Silent, mostly; pairs clatter their bills at the nest.
Fact
The gap in its bill grips apple snails. When the invasive golden apple snail spread through Thai rice fields, openbills boomed; colonies of tens of thousands now nest in Thailand.

Green-tailed Sunbird

นกกินปลีหางยาวเขียว · Aethopyga nipalensis

nok kin-plii haang yao khiao · 14 cm

Listen for
Thin tzip notes and a fast twitter among the rhododendrons.
Fact
It lives near the summit of Doi Inthanon. The Inthanon birds are their own race, angkanensis, named for Doi Ang Ka, the mountain's old name, and the only race with a scarlet breast band.

Barn Owl

นกแสก · Tyto javanica

nok saek · 34 cm

Listen for
No hoot: a long, rasping shriek in the dark.
Fact
The heart-shaped face is a dish that funnels sound to the ears, and one ear sits higher than the other, so it can pin a mouse in total darkness. A family eats thousands of rats a year, so farmers put up nest boxes. Around Chiang Mai it is the eastern barn owl; the photograph and recording here are of its western cousin, Tyto alba.
Story
In Thailand a barn owl landing on a house is read as a sign of death or bad luck, and the family makes merit to turn it away. The face drawn here is the heart curve: x = 16 sin³t, y = 13 cos t − 5 cos 2t − 2 cos 3t − cos 4t.

Eurasian Tree Sparrow

นกกระจอกบ้าน · Passer montanus

nok kra-jok baan · 14 cm

Listen for
Cheerful chip, chip, and a roar of chatter at the evening roost.
Fact
In Asia this is the town sparrow, doing the job the house sparrow does in Europe.
Story
At temples, small cages of sparrows are sold to set free for merit, ปล่อยนก. Most of the birds are caught wild for it.

Who sings when

Each ring is one bird; its thickness is how much it sings at that hour, shaped to today's sunrise and sunset in Chiang Mai. The red dashes are civil dawn and dusk. Drag the hand round the day, then press play to hear that hour. The curves follow field-guide habits; they are a sketch of a typical day.

Birds of the Himmapan

The Himmapan is the forest at the foot of Mount Meru, home of the mixed creatures painted on temple walls and carved on gables across Lanna.

Takeaway · CC BY-SA 3.0

Hatsadiling

นกหัสดีลิงค์

A bird of the Himmapan with an elephant's head. In Lanna, a high monk or a royal may be cremated on a pyre built as a hatsadiling. In 2008 the pyre for Chan Kusalo, head of the northern sangha, stood at Wat Chedi Luang. Wat Chedi Luang

Hamsa

หงส์

The goose of Brahma and the emblem of Hongsawadi, the Mon kingdom at Bago. Mon and Burmese-style temples raise a hamsa on top of a tall pole.

Karawek

การเวก

A bird whose song is so sweet that every creature that hears it stops to listen. Drawn here as rings of sound.

Garuda

ครุฑ

Half man, half eagle: Vishnu's mount and the enemy of the nagas. The garuda is the emblem on Thai government papers.

Kinnari

กินรี

Half woman, half bird. Manohra, a kinnari princess, marries Prince Suthon in a tale told from Lanna to the south. In the north, Shan (Tai Yai) dancers perform the kinnara dance, ฟ้อนนกกิ่งกะหรา, with wings and a tail strapped on.
